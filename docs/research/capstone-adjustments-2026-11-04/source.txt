P0042 [Heading 1] INTRODUCTION

P0043 [Heading 2] 1.1 Project Context

P0044 [Normal] Effective inventory management remains one of the most significant challenges in retail businesses, particularly in stores that experience high daily transaction volumes and frequent inventory movement. Shalom Store in Tangub City processes an average of 400 customer  transactions per day, manages approximately 1,200 active products, and replenishes its inventory twice a week to meet customer demand. In addition, the store offers a wide variety of merchandise across multiple product categories, including groceries, beverages, canned goods, snacks, personal care products, school and office supplies, and other consumer goods. The broad assortment of products, combined with frequent customer transactions and regular inventory replenishment, increases the complexity of monitoring inventory levels, identifying fast-moving products, and maintaining sufficient stock availability.

P0045 [Normal] For the purposes of this study, a high-volume department store is operationally defined as a retail establishment that experiences a high frequency of daily customer transactions, manages a large number of active products, offers merchandise across multiple product categories, and requires frequent inventory replenishment due to continuous product movement. Based on this operational definition, Shalom Store was selected as the study site because it processes approximately 400 customer transactions daily, maintains around 1,200 active products, carries a diverse range of merchandise, and restocks its inventory twice a week. Although the store operates on a smaller scale than large national retail chains, its operational characteristics and inventory management requirements are comparable to those of a high-volume department store, making it an appropriate environment for implementing and evaluating the proposed AI-driven inventory management and demand forecasting system.

P0046 [Normal] Like many retail businesses, the store faces challenges such as stock-outs, overstocking, delayed inventory updates, and difficulty predicting future product demand. These issues may result in lost sales opportunities, increased inventory holding costs, inefficient replenishment decisions, and reduced customer satisfaction. According to Blair (2023), inventory distortion caused by stock-outs, overstocking, and inventory shrinkage was projected to cost retailers approximately $1.77 trillion globally in 2023, emphasizing the importance of adopting more effective inventory management strategies.

P0047 [Normal] If these inventory management problems remain unresolved, retailers may continue to experience lost sales opportunities, unnecessary inventory holding costs, inefficient stock replenishment, and poor customer service. Managers often struggle to make timely inventory decisions because they rely on outdated or incomplete inventory information, particularly during periods of fluctuating customer demand. These challenges become even more critical in high-volume department stores where frequent inventory transactions and continuous stock movement make manual monitoring increasingly difficult and prone to human error.

P0048 [Normal] Recent studies suggest that Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning have become effective technologies for improving inventory management. According to Sekhar (2024), AI significantly enhances demand forecasting, stock optimization, and automated replenishment by analyzing historical sales records, seasonal demand, market trends, and customer purchasing behavior. Similarly, machine learning techniques enable forecasting models to learn from historical sales data and continuously improve prediction accuracy, allowing retailers to anticipate future demand more effectively and make data-driven inventory decisions.

P0049 [Normal] Despite the availability of computerized inventory systems, many existing solutions primarily focus on inventory recording, stock monitoring, and report generation while providing limited predictive capabilities for future demand. This study aims to develop RetailMind: An AI-Driven Inventory Management and Demand Forecasting System for High-Volume Department Stores for Shalom Store in Tangub City. Specifically, the system seeks to analyze historical sales data, generate demand forecasts, provide low-stock notifications, and assist store personnel in making timely, data-driven inventory decisions that reduce stock-outs and overstocking while improving operational efficiency.

P0050 [Normal] The proposed web-based system integrates a Random Forest Regressor  as its demand forecasting model. RFR is a supervised ensemble machine-learning algorithm that builds multiple decision trees using samples of the available data and combines their predictions to produce a final demand estimate. This approach enables the model to identify complex and nonlinear relationships among historical sales , sales trends, days of the week, months, and relevant calendar-based events. By combining the predictions of multiple trees, the model can provide stable forecasts while reducing the possibility that individual data patterns will excessively influence the results.

P0051 [Normal] The Random Forest Regressor was selected because retail demand can be affected by interacting factors and may vary across products, categories, dates, and seasonal periods. Its ability to process several predictor variables and identify complex sales patterns makes it suitable for Shalom Store’s diverse inventory and transaction data. The forecasting model may be periodically retrained using newly accumulated sales records so that its predictions remain responsive to changing demand patterns. Its generated forecasts and replenishment recommendations will function as decision-support information, while final restocking decisions will remain under the control of authorized Inventory Managers.

P0052 [Normal] The proposed system also contributes to the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals by supporting Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure under SDG 9 through the application of artificial intelligence and digital technologies to modernize retail inventory operations. It advances Responsible Consumption and Production under SDG 12 by providing demand forecasts and replenishment recommendations that can help reduce unnecessary overstocking, stock shortages, and potential product waste. Furthermore, RetailMind supports Decent Work and Economic Growth under SDG 8 by improving operational efficiency and assisting store personnel in making timely, informed, and data-driven inventory decisions.

P0053 [Normal] Through real-time inventory monitoring, Random Forest-based demand forecasting, low-stock notifications, inventory analytics, and replenishment recommendations, RetailMind aims to reduce stock-outs and overstocking, improve inventory accuracy, and support timely inventory planning. The proposed system is expected to provide Shalom Store with a more organized, data-driven, and efficient approach to managing its inventory operations.

P0054 [Heading 2] 1.2 Purpose and Description

P0055 [Normal] This project aims to develop a web-based AI-driven inventory management and demand forecasting system designed specifically for Shalom Store, a high-volume department store in Tangub City. The proposed system seeks to address the challenges associated with manual inventory monitoring and optimize inventory operations by providing a centralized platform for managing inventory records, monitoring stock movements, and forecasting future product demand using artificial intelligence.

P0056 [Normal] RetailMind: An AI-Driven Inventory Management and Demand Forecasting System for High-Volume Department Store is intended to improve inventory management by enabling store personnel to monitor stock levels in real time, analyze historical sales data, and receive demand forecasting insights that support timely inventory replenishment decisions. The system functions as a decision support system by providing Inventory Managers with data-driven forecasts and replenishment recommendations while leaving the final inventory decisions to authorized personnel. The forecasting model will initially start collecting store sales data for a period of time due for the reason that there is no available store historical sale data for the initial set up and are designed to be periodically updated as new transaction data become available, allowing the system to improve its demand forecasting capability over time. Through this predictive capability, RetailMind aims to reduce stock-outs, minimize overstocking, improve inventory accuracy, and support informed decision-making. The generated forecasts serve as recommendations rather than automatic decisions, assisting Inventory Managers in evaluating replenishment needs, maintaining appropriate stock levels, and improving overall operational efficiency

P0057 [Normal] This system will serve as a web-based information system that centralizes inventory-related activities and provides authorized users with convenient access to inventory information. By integrating inventory management with AI-based demand forecasting, the proposed system aims to provide Shalom Store with a more efficient, accurate, and intelligent approach to managing inventory while supporting better operational planning, inventory control, and customer service.

P0091 [Heading 2] 1.4 Scope and Limitations of the Project

P0092 [Normal] Shalom Store is a high-volume retail establishment located in Tangub City that processes approximately 400 customer transactions daily, manages around 1,200 active products across multiple categories, and replenishes its inventory twice a week. RetailMind: An AI-Driven Inventory Management and Demand Forecasting System for High-Volume Department Store is a web-based system designed specifically for the store’s inventory operations and is accessible through desktop and mobile web browsers.

P0093 [Normal] The system supports supports four role workspaces for platform governance, Store administration, inventory management and cashier operations. Its scope covers product and batch inventory, direct stock receiving, physical counts, Stock Issue review, cashier shifts, sales, Receipt History, authorized refunds and corrections, manual GCash services, reports, notifications and audit records. Manual GCash services record reference numbers, fees and internal cash and wallet reconciliation. Staff perform the external GCash transfer separately. Cost-based profit reporting depends on recorded purchase costs.

P0094 [Normal] The Random Forest pipeline estimates recorded sales through a 28-day horizon, with evaluation summaries for 7, 14 and 28-day totals. The current feature set excludes category attributes, prices, promotions and local-event indicators. Forecasts and stock-rule suggestions remain advisory and are labeled by their basis. Training, quality checks, technical and operational review and completed observation evidence are separate from history-readiness labels.

P0095 [Normal] The project is limited to the inventory management and demand forecasting operations of Shalom Store. Forecast accuracy depends on the availability, consistency, completeness, and representativeness of the historical sales data. Sudden changes in customer behavior, natural disasters, market disruptions, supplier shortages, theft, or damaged inventory may reduce forecasting accuracy. The local installation depends on the Store PC, server, database and configured network. Internet access is required for external services such as email and externally hosted assets. Finalized sales remain historical records, while authorized refunds and corrections use linked records rather than silently replacing the original transaction.

P0104 [Heading 2] 1.6 Definition of Terms

P0105 [Normal] The following terms are defined conceptually and operationally as they are used within the context of this study:

P0106 [Normal] Artificial Intelligence (AI). It refers to computer technology that performs tasks requiring intelligent decision-making. This refers to RetailMind's capability to analyze historical sales data and generate demand forecasts using a Random Forest Regressor.

P0107 [Normal] Barcode Scanner. It refers to an electronic device used to read barcode labels for product identification. This refers to the device used in RetailMind to record stock-in, stock-out, and sales transactions accurately.

P0108 [Normal] Demand Forecasting. It refers to the process of estimating future customer demand using historical data. In this implementation, demand forecasting estimates expected recorded product sales for replenishment planning; it does not directly measure all customer demand lost during shortages.

P0109 [Normal] Inventory Management. It refers to the process of monitoring and controlling product inventory. This refers to RetailMind's functions for tracking stock levels, recording inventory movements, and managing inventory records.

P0110 [Normal] Machine Learning (ML). It refers to a field of artificial intelligence that enables systems to learn from historical data. This refers to the technology used by RetailMind to identify sales patterns and generate demand forecasts.

P0111 [Normal] Overstock. It refers to a condition in which inventory exceeds customer demand. This refers to products identified by RetailMind as having stock levels higher than forecasted demand.

P0112 [Normal] Random Forest Regressor (RFR). It refers to a supervised ensemble machine-learning algorithm that combines predictions from multiple decision trees to estimate a continuous numerical value. In RetailMind, the Random Forest Regressor uses product identity, forecast step, sales lags, rolling sales statistics and target-date indicators to estimate recorded sales in Base Units.

P0113 [Normal] Replenishment. It refers to the process of restoring inventory to the required stock level. This refers to RetailMind's recommendations for restocking products based on forecasted demand and current inventory levels.

P0114 [Normal] RetailMind. It refers to an AI-driven inventory management and demand forecasting system. This refers to the proposed web-based system that monitors inventory, records stock transactions, and forecasts product demand for Shalom Store.

P0115 [Normal] Stock-out. It refers to a condition in which a product is unavailable due to insufficient inventory. This refers to RetailMind's detection of low or zero stock levels and generation of low-stock alerts.

P0132 [Normal] Comparative evaluations of retail demand forecasting models further justify the use of ensemble tree-based algorithms over traditional linear and standalone statistical models. In the M5 Forecasting accuracy benchmark analyzed by Makridakis et al. (2022), machine learning methods that combine lagged sales series, rolling statistical aggregates, and calendar variables consistently outperformed traditional univariate time-series approaches across multi-item retail datasets. Unlike Multiple Linear Regression (MLR), which assumes a strictly linear relationship between predictors and target demand, or Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA), which requires training and tuning a separate parameter set for every individual product, a global Random Forest Regressor (RFR) learns cross-product demand behaviors simultaneously within a single unified model.

P0133 [Normal] Similarly, empirical retail forecasting studies by Googerdchi and Jafari (2025) and Zavaleta-Zarate et al. (2026) highlight that the Random Forest Regressor mitigates overfitting in noisy daily retail transactions through bootstrap aggregating (bagging). Because daily retail logs frequently exhibit zero-sale days interspersed with sudden multi-unit purchases, single decision trees and linear models are highly sensitive to transaction variance. By constructing an ensemble of decorrelated decision trees and averaging their predictions across multi-day horizons (such as 14-day and 28-day replenishment windows), RFR smooths out daily transaction noise and provides stable demand estimates suitable for physical store restocking.

P0272 [Normal] The figure illustrates the existing inventory management process employed by Shalom Store prior to the development of the proposed RetailMind system. When a customer purchases a product, the cashier records the transaction using a cash register; however, inventory quantities are not automatically updated. As a result, the existing manual process is time-consuming, prone to human error, and may lead to stock-outs, overstocking, inaccurate inventory records, and delays in inventory-related decision-making.

P0369 [Heading 3] 3.4.2 Machine Learning Training Pipeline

P0370 [Normal] To validate the predictive capability of the Random Forest Regressor (RFR) before live store activation, the proponents implemented a reproducible training and evaluation pipeline in Google Colab using Python, pandas (v2.2.3), numpy, pyarrow, and scikit-learn. Because Shalom Store previously relied on manual physical logbooks without digitized item-level daily sales histories, pre-deployment model benchmarking was conducted using a pinned subset of the public M5 retail dataset (50 products from the CA_1 FOODS retail store category across 730 training days from March 1, 2014 to February 28, 2016), while enforcing a strict integration boundary so that public benchmark data remain completely isolated from Shalom Store's production database.

P0371 [Normal] The machine learning method processes historical sales records to predict the target variable units_sold across 7-day, 14-day, and 28-day forecasting horizons using 14 engineered predictor features:

T009 [TABLE] Feature Category | Engineered Feature Name(s) | Count | Operational Purpose in Demand Forecasting

P0375 [Normal] To prevent temporal data leakage—where future sales information inadvertently influence	past predictions during random shuffling—the pipeline enforces a strict Rolling-Origin Chronological Validation split . The model is trained on two years of historical daily sales (730 days), the subsequent two 28-day chronological blocks are used solely as validation folds to calibrate the sale-day classification threshold (set at 0.5), and the final held-out 28-day window serves as the unseen Testing Set. To ensure that the AI model provides measurable value over simple rule-based estimation, RFR predictions are benchmarked directly against a Seasonal Naive Baseline ('predict the exact same unit sales recorded on the same day last week') across 7-day, 14-day, and 28-day horizons.

P0615 [Normal] 4.3.2.1 Forecast Accuracy Testing Results (Google Colab Training & Validation)

P0616 [Normal] Prior to activating live store forecasts, the proponents executed the demand forecasting experiment in Google Colab (m5_sales_experiment.py) using 50 retail products over a two-year training window (March 1, 2014 to February 28, 2016, totaling 730 days) and evaluated the trained Random Forest Regressor (RFR) on the final unseen 28-day test window (Fold 3). The model was trained using the 14 engineered predictor variables (product_id, horizon_day, lag_1, lag_7, lag_14, lag_28, rolling_mean_7, rolling_std_7, rolling_mean_28, rolling_std_28, day_of_week, day_of_month, month, and is_weekend) and compared directly against the Seasonal Naive baseline (predicting the previous week's sales). Table 12 presents the comparative forecast accuracy results across the 7-day, 14-day, and 28-day test horizons.

P0622 [Normal] As shown in Table 12, the forecasting evaluation reveals an important operational insight across replenishment horizons:

P0623 [Normal] The public M5 holdout provides benchmark evidence for the selected products. Seasonal Naive recorded lower error at 7 days, while Random Forest recorded lower error at 14 and 28 days. At 28 days, reported WAPE was 29.63% for Random Forest and 36.53% for Seasonal Naive. Aggregate results can mask weak forecasts for individual products. Any-sale-day classification measures the identification of sale days, and a missed sale day does not establish that a stock-out occurred.

P0624 [Normal] RetailMind classifies Store history by usable days: Insufficient below 56, Low from 56 to 179, Medium from 180 to 364 and High at 365 or more. Closures, stock-outs and missing evidence are handled separately. Candidate qualification uses chronological evaluation against the baseline and configured quality gates. Prospective observation and local Administrator approval are separate requirements.

P0629 [Normal] Per-Product Error Analysis, Model Limitations, and Integration Safeguards

P0630 [Normal] While the Random Forest Regressor outperformed the baseline across the aggregate 50-product portfolio on the 14-day and 28-day horizons (improving 28-day absolute error over the baseline for 28 out of 50 products), per-product diagnostic inspection revealed that RFR did not beat the simple baseline for 22 out of 50 products. This finding justifies two critical architectural safeguards built directly into the RetailMind web application:

P0631 [Normal] 1. Forecast Exceptions Queue & WAPE Alert Threshold (35%): Because aggregate metrics can mask weak predictions on individual erratic items, RetailMind includes a dedicated Forecast Exceptions module. Any product whose validation WAPE exceeds the 35% threshold or fails to beat the 7-day baseline is automatically flagged in the exception queue for manual review by the Inventory Manager.

P0632 [Normal] 2. Operational Data Readiness & Stock-Out Blind-Spot Mitigation: Pure historical sales records cannot distinguish between zero customer demand and zero shelf inventory (stock-outs). Unlike public benchmark datasets that lack physical stock-out logs, RetailMind's MySQL database natively records real-time inventory balances, stock issue reports, Cost/Sales prices, promotions, and Store Calendar closures. In production at Shalom Store, the public M5 demonstration data remain strictly isolated; local model activation requires accumulating sufficient store transaction history across the system's Data Readiness bands (<56 days: Insufficient; 56–179 days: Low; 180–364 days: Medium; 365+ days: High) and passing chronological validation before assisting the Inventory Manager with replenishment decisions.

P0637 [Heading 2] 5.1 SUMMARY

P0639 [Normal] RetailMind integrates inventory, receiving, checkout and forecasting workflows for Shalom Store. Four workspaces separate platform governance, Store administration, inventory management and cashier operations. The system maintains traceable stock movements, saved receipts, linked refunds and operational records. Buying guidance combines eligible forecast output or stock rules with recorded inventory information to support decisions by authorized personnel.

P0640 [Normal] The public M5 benchmark showed that Random Forest produced lower error than Seasonal Naive at the reported 14- and 28-day horizons, while Seasonal Naive performed better at 7 days. Isolated tests demonstrated the operation of the forecasting pipeline. The reported evidence for installed unattended operation and predictive usefulness on genuine Store outcomes was still incomplete.

P0641 [Heading 2] 5.2 CONCLUSION

P0642 [Normal] Based on the development, pre-deployment model validation, and operational testing of RetailMind for Shalom Store in Tangub City, the proponents formulated the following conclusions:

P0643 [Normal] Transitioning Shalom Store from manual physical logbooks to a centralized, role-based web application across four workspaces (Super Administrator, Administrator, Inventory Manager, and Cashier) streamlines barcode-assisted POS checkout, real-time stock monitoring, direct stock receiving, physical inventory counts, and manual GCash transaction logging.

P0644 [Normal] Pre-deployment benchmarking of the 14-feature Random Forest Regressor (RFR) pipeline in Google Colab using a 50-product subset of the M5 retail dataset across 730 training days demonstrated that while the Seasonal Naive baseline yielded lower error on noisy 7-day horizons (37.88% vs. 43.00% WAPE), the Random Forest Regressor outperformed the baseline on 14-day (32.44% vs. 37.71% WAPE) and 28-day (29.63% vs. 36.53% WAPE) replenishment horizons, while achieving a 93.14% recall (0.7525 F1-Score) in active sale-day classification.

P0645 [Normal] Integrating architectural safeguards—specifically the Forecast Data Readiness bands (<56 days: Insufficient up to 365+ days: High), the 35% WAPE Forecast Exceptions queue, and Stock-Rule Replenishment Suggestions—ensures that Shalom Store receives reliable inventory buying guidance during the initial cold-start data collection phase without contaminating live store records with external benchmark data.

P0646 [Heading 2] 5.3 RECOMMENDATION

P0647 [Normal] Based on the findings and limitations of the study, the following recommendations are proposed:

P0648 [Normal] Continuous POS Data Accumulation for Local Model Activation: Shalom Store personnel should consistently record all daily checkout transactions, stock receiving entries, and physical counts in RetailMind so products can progress past the 56-day Data Readiness threshold and activate live 14-day and 28-day Random Forest demand forecasts.

P0649 [Normal] Focus on 14-Day and 28-Day Replenishment Horizons: Store management should utilize the 14-day and 28-day forecast summaries alongside Stock-Rule Suggestions for bi-weekly and monthly purchasing decisions, while using the Forecast Exceptions module to manually review erratic items exceeding the 35% WAPE threshold.

P0650 [Normal] Future Feature Expansion: Future researchers and developers may expand the 14-feature forecasting pipeline by incorporating recorded product category attributes, unit price changes, active store promotions, and local Tangub City calendar events once sufficient multi-year store transaction history has been accumulated.
