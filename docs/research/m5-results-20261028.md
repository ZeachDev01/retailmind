## Interpretation and weaknesses

Random Forest fails to improve 28-day absolute error over baseline for 9/20 products. Inspect per-product scores; aggregate improvement can hide weak products.
High sale-day F1 can come from frequently positive sales. Compare with always_sale; it does not establish quantity accuracy or stockout detection.
Only one final 28-day test window is available; two training years contain few annual cycles. Walmart sales and US calendar effects do not establish local Store performance.
Recorded zeros do not identify stockouts or lost demand. The shared model currently uses sales lags and calendar fields, not prices, promotions, or stock availability.

## Improvements to investigate

1. Inspect worst product/horizon errors and sustained over/underprediction in daily_predictions.csv.
2. Use validation to test price/promotion features only when known at the forecast origin; do not use realized future prices or tune against this final test.
3. Evaluate additional future windows and locally collected Store history before operational approval.

## Integration boundary

Collect actual Store sales first. Train an isolated candidate when complete usable history and chronological validation are sufficient; retain review/approval before daily forecast generation. Public M5 history and models stay separate. Forecasts support replenishment and never change stock.
The existing readiness bands are below 56 days: Insufficient; 56–179: Low; 180–364: Medium; 365+: High only with acceptable validation. Minimum feature history is not sufficient evidence for reliable validation or automatic approval.
No production activation or accuracy pass is inferred from this experiment.

## Recorded interpretation — 2026-10-28

This is a **local CPU run**, not a Colab execution. The self-contained [Colab notebook](m5_random_forest_colab.ipynb) is prepared, but browser control is unavailable in this session. Reproduce the run there before reporting Colab as the runtime. Work is on `experiment/m5-colab`; the operational database and forecasting implementation were not modified.

- The final test covers **2016-04-25 through 2016-05-22**. Validation origins are 2016-02-28 and 2016-03-27. Final training may use earlier observed validation sales; it never uses final-test labels.
- At 7 days, model MAE **8.134** exceeds baseline **7.250**: about **12.19% worse**. At 14 days it improves MAE by about **6.60%**; at 28 days by about **5.88%**. The gains are modest and depend on the horizon.
- The model predicts **1,521.76 units** against **1,845 actual units** across the final 28-day test: an aggregate underforecast of **323.24 units (17.52%)**. This suggests investigating replenishment underestimates, not interpreting a single F1 as overall accuracy.
- Nine of 20 products fail to improve 28-day absolute error. `FOODS_3_477` is an example: model error **66.85 units**, baseline **21.00**. Other regressions include `FOODS_3_123` (**19.90 vs 3.00**) and `FOODS_3_609` (**29.40 vs 15.00**). Check trend changes and daily patterns before selecting a remedy.
- **30.89%** of final-test product/days have zero recorded sales. The model's sale-day recall is **89.92%**, but precision is **69.05%**, with **156 false positives** and only **17 true negatives**. Its F1 **0.7811** loses to always predicting a sale (**0.8173**). High recall alone does not justify quantity forecasts or stockout claims.
- Under the existing Demo settings, this run would fail the 35% WAPE cap at 7 and 28 days and the baseline comparison at 7 days. Those settings are a reference, not an agreed M5 or operational acceptance policy. Keep the failed evidence; do not lower thresholds after seeing this test.

Next experiments should be designed on validation windows. Inspect products with persistent underforecasting, compare performance across additional chronological windows, and then test useful features that are available at each forecast origin. This final test is now observed: tuning based on it makes it development evidence; a future confirmation needs fresh held-out data.
