## Problem Statement

The Store needs Demand Forecasting to work reliably on its own Windows PC using XAMPP and localhost. The developer Super Administrator will not have routine access after deployment. The Store usually closes around 9 PM on weekdays, closes earlier on weekends, and does not always leave the PC powered or plugged in overnight. Forecasting must therefore recover when the PC starts instead of depending on someone opening a browser, signing into RetailMind, starting XAMPP manually, or calling the developer.

The existing pipeline can queue training and generation, fit a Random Forest, persist results, and suggest replenishment. However, uninterrupted operation is not established: missed schedules are not fully caught up, ordinary failures do not receive bounded retries, notifications are incomplete, and stale forecasts can influence purchasing quantities despite stricter checks on displayed expected sales. Several model settings shown in the interface do not control the settings actually used by the worker. Real Store activation and recovery do not form a complete local staff workflow.

The Store also needs honest sales evidence. A Product selling nothing may have been available with no purchases, unavailable for part of the day, or missing reliable records. Positive sales before a stockout do not establish the full amount customers wanted. Current closing-stock checks can miss intraday stockouts. Requiring 140 consecutive usable days can repeatedly exclude Products rather than retaining usable history accumulated before a gap.

Finally, the user wants to know whether historical selling prices and promotions improve forecasting. Adding columns is not itself evidence of value. The current model has 14 input columns and does not use stockout, selling-price, or promotion inputs. M5 provides useful historical selling prices, but the selected source does not contain verified stockout or product-promotion flags. Production reliability must not wait for unavailable experimental evidence or imply that local Store forecast accuracy is already proven.

## Solution

Deliver a mostly unattended local forecasting workflow. Windows starts the required local services and scheduling; daily Forecast Generation and weekly candidate Model Retraining use completed Philippine calendar days. Missed work is caught up on startup, temporary failures receive at most three total attempts, and clear local notices explain exhausted retries or unmet prerequisites. Cashier and inventory operations continue independently of forecasting.

The local Administrator reviews and approves models only after fixed automated checks pass. The Super Administrator handles initial technical configuration and later technical maintenance, rather than routine approval. Failed candidates leave the approved model unchanged. Observation, dataset isolation, audit history, compatible rollback, and explicit recommendation suspension remain enforced. Products without trustworthy current forecasts use clearly labelled stock rules.

Count at least 140 accumulated usable daily records per Product without resetting the count at a stockout or unknown day. Keep original dates, identify incomplete evidence honestly, and require sufficient chronological evaluation coverage before a Product can receive approved model guidance. Eligibility, evaluation success, local approval, and forecast availability are separate states.

Group forecasting pages under a dedicated Forecasting navigation group for both Administrator roles, in workflow order. Reuse existing pages and services, completing the local review and recovery actions that are currently missing.

Deliver reliable operation and the local approval workflow first. Then run an isolated, reproducible historical-price experiment against the current sales-and-calendar model and weekly Seasonal Naive baseline. Collect genuine stockout and promotion evidence for later experiments. Additional inputs enter an approved Store model only after the agreed evidence checks and local approval; public dataset results never establish Store accuracy.

## Implementation Decisions

### Architecture and delivery order

- Keep one asynchronous, horizon-conditioned Random Forest pipeline. PHP records jobs and displays persisted results; the scheduled Python CLI worker performs training and generation. Reuse the existing scheduler, history reader, queue, candidate evaluation, activation, replenishment, and attention modules. Do not introduce a second forecasting service or synchronous request-time training.
- Deliver in this order: reliable local operation and staff workflow; isolated historical-price evaluation; later stockout/promotion feature experiments once genuine evidence exists. Recording evidence belongs to pipeline preparation, but unproven features must not be switched on simply because columns are available.
- Apply the local Administrator approval decision recorded in ADR-0022. It replaces the Super Administrator-only routine approval boundary in the earlier forecasting design. Preserve the rest of the single-pipeline, observation, security, isolation, and advisory-guidance requirements.
- This spec authorizes implementation of its future behavior; it does not claim that inspected development code already implements it or that a candidate is approved.

### Local deployment, scheduling, and recovery

- Target a Windows Store PC running XAMPP locally. Resolve and retain the actual PHP executable, supported Python environment, project location, database connection, model storage, and service account during installation. Do not rely on Laragon paths, an interactive user's PATH, a mapped drive, or a control-panel window staying open.
- Configure required local services and Windows scheduling to start without an interactive Windows sign-in. Use a narrowly scoped local account and the permissions needed for database access, logs, and model artifacts. Keep the database and training interface local; do not add remote developer access.
- Daily Forecast Generation is due at 01:00 Asia/Manila. Weekly candidate training is due Sunday at 01:00 Asia/Manila and requires new usable history. Schedule times are real persisted settings consumed by the scheduler, with the agreed values as defaults.
- Forecast origins and training cutoffs use the latest completed Philippine calendar day. At a morning startup, yesterday is eligible; today's partial sales are not. A powered-off period does not prove the Store was closed or had zero sales.
- Record successful work and outstanding due work by purpose and evidence cutoff, rather than deduplicating only by enqueue date or treating a failed job as fulfilled work. Repeated or concurrent ticks must not enqueue duplicate work for the same logical run.
- After downtime, queue one current forecast instead of recreating every missed daily forecast. Catch up missed weekly training once if new eligible history exists, without duplicating an existing queued/running logical run. If both are due, generate with the currently approved model and train a separate candidate; training is not automatic approval.
- Check service readiness after startup and retry background startup checks when the database is still starting. The system must become visibly unhealthy if readiness does not recover, rather than remaining silently idle because its own database was unavailable for the first check.
- Reuse the job queue and lease mechanism. Temporary execution failures receive at most three total attempts per logical job, including crash/lease recovery; use later scheduler ticks rather than a busy retry loop. Do not create a new logical job to bypass the attempt limit. A retry must not race a still-valid worker lease or duplicate results.
- Treat invalid configuration, incompatible artifacts, missing approval, insufficient history, and unavailable evaluation prerequisites as explicit blocked/not-ready conditions. Do not repeatedly retrain or retry them as though they were transient connection failures. Reconsider a blocked condition when its underlying prerequisite changes or an authorized operator requests a retry after correction.
- Every run has a visible queued/running/succeeded/failed or equivalent blocked/not-ready outcome, cutoff, model identity where applicable, attempt count, timestamps, safe operator explanation, and technical diagnostic reference. Claims of successful completion require committed complete results.
- Report exhausted retries and persistent background inactivity to the local Administrator through existing attention/notification surfaces. Match failure notices to queue failures as well as training-run failures; deduplicate notices for the same incident. Preserve a local diagnostic when database failure prevents immediate in-app reporting, and surface it after recovery.
- Forecasting failure, suspension, candidate training, and observation must not block checkout, refunds, Stock Receiving, inventory counts, or stock-rule suggestions.

### Sales evidence, stockouts, and Product eligibility

- Keep the prediction target as expected corrected recorded sales in Base Units. The feature-value experiment does not change the target into estimated lost sales or unconstrained customer demand.
- Retain completed sales and their historical selling form, Base Unit conversion, approved reversals, and Cash Refund corrections. Later corrections affect only origins at which they were available. Forecast classifications must not mutate financial or stock records.
- A Stockout Day means recorded evidence shows a Product unavailable during part or all of Store trading. Its recorded sales remain valid operational records, but it is not a complete observation of customer demand. Do not interpret its zero sales as proof of zero demand or invent the number of customers who could not buy.
- Reconstruct a Product's availability from its known starting balance and ordered, dated stock movements over the relevant trading interval, using existing Store Calendar information. A zero/nonpositive balance after sales or other movements is not hidden by later restocking. Positive closing stock alone is insufficient proof of all-day availability.
- If the starting balance, movement completeness, trading interval, or historical cutoff cannot establish availability, report unknown availability. Do not infer a stockout merely from a zero overnight balance while the Store was closed, nor infer all-day availability from a first late-day receipt. Keep evidence boundaries visible instead of claiming precise stockout duration that the records cannot establish.
- Distinguish observed sales, observed zero sales with supported availability, Stockout Days, closures, missing evidence, and future/uncompleted days. Even supported zero sales are observed zero purchases, not a claim that nobody wanted the Product.
- Existing certified historical sales imports remain valid recorded-sales evidence, with POS precedence and provenance. An imported zero lacking availability evidence does not automatically certify an unconstrained genuine-zero day. Do not rewrite existing imports or demand fabricated stock evidence to preserve recorded sales; expose the distinction and apply the conservative eligibility/evaluation policy.
- Require at least 140 accumulated usable daily records per Product for candidate-training eligibility. A supported zero-sales day counts. A Stockout Day, closure, incomplete day, or unknown day does not count, but does not erase records accumulated before it. Twenty calendar weeks are only the earliest possible minimum; gaps can make accumulation take longer.
- Preserve original calendar dates and cutoff revisions. Do not remove excluded dates and then pretend the remaining rows are consecutive days. Do not forward-fill unknown demand or invent zero labels to satisfy eligibility, lag calculations, or holdouts.
- Usable-day count is a prerequisite, not sufficient proof of training/evaluation readiness. Construct valid dated feature windows and observed targets; skip unsupported Product/origin combinations with a recorded reason. Use explicit missingness or omit unsupported inputs according to the feature contract, never hidden zero demand. Where the deployed model cannot accept the available recent feature window, that Product remains on stock rules until a valid current input exists.
- Evaluate on three chronological 28-day holdouts. Only complete, usable Product/horizon totals may be compared as full 7-, 14-, or 28-day totals; do not compare a partial total to a full-horizon prediction. Report included/excluded Products and periods, preserve matching populations across models/baseline, and block approval when required horizon/holdout evidence is missing. One Product's missing evidence must not abort all other eligible Products.
- Keep Forecast Readiness separate from the 140-day eligibility threshold and model approval. Retain existing readiness vocabulary and validation-dependent bands without implying a probability of accuracy. Display the actual accumulated usable-day count and exclusion reasons.

### Operational input contract and evidence capture

- The existing 14 model input columns are product_id, horizon_day, lag_1, lag_7, lag_14, lag_28, rolling_mean_7, rolling_std_7, rolling_mean_28, rolling_std_28, day_of_week, day_of_month, month, and is_weekend. Product identity is encoded for training. Lag_1 refers to the origin-day observation; lags and rolling summaries are historical, while calendar fields describe the target day.
- The history contract also carries date, Product identity, corrected units_sold, availability/version timing, and evidence classification/provenance. These control reproducibility and are not automatically extra predictors.
- Extend the shared evidence/export contract to preserve nullable out_of_stock, price, and is_on_promotion evidence with effective/observation dates, recorded/available timestamps, and source. Unknown is distinct from zero stockout, zero price, and no promotion. Consumers must not silently strip these fields or treat their presence as approval to use them as predictors.
- Price means the dated normal selling price per Base Unit; retain the distinction from purchase cost, pack selling price, and promotion discount. Historical transaction prices have transaction-only coverage and require the recorded selling-form conversion. Do not use today's Product price to backfill earlier days.
- Capture current selling-price evidence during ordinary Product creation, relevant receiving/catalog updates, and CSV changes through the existing price-driver mechanism. Preserve historical revisions and write capture consistently with the business update, rather than letting a successful edit silently omit its evidence. Do not invent earlier prices where tracking did not exist.
- Retain promotion effective dates, scope, identity, scheduling time, changes, and cancellation. is_on_promotion means a scheduled applicable offer exists, not proof that a Cashier selected it or that every sale received a discount. Preserve promotion-specific identity so cancelling one offer does not erase another applicable offer.
- Resolve driver revisions deterministically by relevant type, identity, effective date, and recorded cutoff. A newer price event must not hide an otherwise applicable promotion. Evidence reads must reproduce the schedules known at the origin, not today's edited promotion state.
- Stockout flags describe historical observations; realized future stockouts must never be supplied as known target-day features. Treat lost demand as unknown. Stockout evidence handling is required even before there is enough variation to establish value as a predictor.
- Start the M5 price experiment with price observations known no later than the origin, using explicitly named historical/last-known semantics and preserving missingness. Do not use realized future selling prices. Future price/promotion predictors can be used only when an effective schedule was recorded before that origin; unknown schedules must not be guessed from later events.
- Change feature-schema/artifact versions when inputs or semantics change. Validate model, dataset, feature schema, and Product compatibility before generation. Preserve the existing approved artifact until a compatible replacement is checked and approved; never silently run an old estimator with a new input shape.

### Candidate checks, local approval, and recovery

- Automatically train candidates with sufficient eligible new evidence; keep scheduled generation bound to the approved model. New Products may join a candidate but do not become operationally forecastable merely by reaching 140 usable days.
- Use chronological rolling-origin validation with labels and input revisions available by each origin. Train reference and candidate recipes using the same permitted past in each fold; do not score a present-day fitted incumbent against an earlier window it has already seen.
- Persist MAE, RMSE, WAPE, comparison population/coverage, and under/overprediction diagnostics per horizon and model version. Quantity metrics compare Product-level horizon totals; WAPE is error, not an accuracy percentage. Evaluate operational quantities after the same nonnegative daily rounding used by the worker, with fractional diagnostics separately labelled if retained.
- Retain the existing configured per-horizon maximum-WAPE gates and requirement to outperform the weekly Seasonal Naive baseline. Configure actual finite, nonnegative limits during initial technical setup, persist the units consistently, and snapshot the effective limits with the evaluation. Do not invent new absolute WAPE limits from this discussion, substitute an unrelated warning threshold, or silently pass when limits/metrics are absent.
- Provide a real technical setup path that writes the settings actually read by scheduler and worker. Fixed horizons, fixed model parameters, and unsupported options must not appear as effective adjustable settings. Clearly show which supported changes require retraining, and keep worker, stored metadata, and UI consistent.
- Without an existing approved Store model, compare the first candidate to the weekly baseline and enforce configured gates. Use stock rules until approval prerequisites are met. Passing an isolated M5 experiment cannot stand in for Store evidence.
- Retain a prospective observation requirement of at least 28 completed target dates backed by usable Store observations for initial Store activation. Generate candidate observation forecasts automatically through the existing non-operational job path; an unapproved candidate must not need promotion merely to begin observation. Missing/Stockout Day evidence does not masquerade as evaluated target coverage.
- Keep observation evidence tied to a specific candidate and genuine forecast origin. Later weekly candidates cannot inherit another model's prospective evidence or backdate predictions. Pending observation must not interrupt generation from an existing approved model. Any retained observation/review prerequisite must be satisfiable without a developer logging in.
- Fixed automated checks supply the technical qualification previously dependent on routine developer technical acceptance. The local Administrator records the operational decision and may approve only qualified candidates. Do not retain a hidden mandatory Super Administrator sign-off that makes this workflow impossible after deployment.
- Add/complete Model Review & Approval for the local Administrator, including candidate identity, approved identity, 7/14/28-day results and limits, observation progress, source/coverage warnings, clear pass/blocked/fail explanations, and approve/reject actions. A passing check is not approval and the action must be disabled and rejected server-side when prerequisites fail.
- Grant the local Administrator only the scoped model review, approval/replacement, pause/resume, and compatible prior-model recovery actions needed for this workflow. This is not general platform governance, arbitrary model uploads, threshold editing, or Super Administrator account access. Preserve existing Workspace isolation, local-only mutation policy, CSRF protection, and server-side capabilities.
- Activation atomically changes the approved model reference and preserves the compatible previous approved model. Record the Administrator, time, candidate, effective gates, and decision as Protected Audit Records. Approval must not immediately relabel old forecast rows as belonging to the new model.
- After activation or compatible rollback, queue a fresh operational forecast for the correct completed-day cutoff. Until that run succeeds, historical output remains dated history and current suggestions use stock rules when model identity or freshness does not match.
- Expose local pause/resume and recovery to the previously approved compatible Store model. A rollback restores a previously qualified artifact without treating it as a new candidate requiring fabricated fresh observation. Refuse absent, incompatible, demo, or otherwise invalid recovery artifacts with a clear reason. Preserve audit history and prevent circular/repeated rollback from overwriting attribution.
- Candidate failure or rejection leaves approved models, previous verified results, and Store records intact. Preserve diagnostic evidence and clean up only artifacts/results proven to belong to a failed attempt; no destructive reset, wholesale history rewrite, or manual database repair is part of ordinary recovery.

### Forecast output and replenishment

- Persist a complete operational run transactionally with Product, forecast origin, 28 distinct ordered target days, model and feature version, daily nonnegative quantities, readiness, limitations, and run identity. Derive 7/14/28-day totals from those same rows. Partial writes are not visible as successful output.
- Gate both displayed expected sales and the actual purchasing calculation on the same usable-forecast rules: successful operational run, complete coverage, current intended target period, correct approved Store model, compatible schema/Product identity, sufficient readiness/input evidence, no observation-only status, and no recommendation suspension.
- A stale, incomplete, superseded, failed, or otherwise ineligible forecast stays available as clearly dated history but cannot drive the current recommended purchase quantity. Use existing stock-rule recommendations instead and label their basis. Retain existing minimum-quantity/package rounding and stock-handling safeguards; this is not a redesign of purchasing policy.
- Forecast Quantity is expected sales in Base Units; Replenishment Recommendation is an advisory purchase suggestion based on stock and planning rules. Neither changes inventory, creates a purchase request/order, or proves customer demand during stockouts.

### Navigation and page workflow

- Create one dedicated Forecasting navigation group for the Super Administrator, removing duplicate forecasting links from unrelated groups. Order destinations as Import Sales History, Data Readiness, ML Operation, Forecast Model Governance, Demand Forecast, and Forecast Exceptions.
- Create a dedicated Forecasting group for the Administrator, ordered Data Readiness, Model Review & Approval, Demand Forecast, and Forecast Exceptions. Link relevant alerts to the exact readiness, approval, or recovery action the Administrator can perform.
- Reuse existing destinations and the current application design. Model Review & Approval completes missing operational functionality rather than introducing another competing training/settings surface. Navigation visibility must match actual backend authorization in each Workspace.
- Keep Store Calendar and Promotions in Store operations and provide contextual links where relevant. System Health remains general platform tooling. Do not duplicate report fragments, alias routes, prototypes, or prepared demo replay as normal production workflow steps; expose replay only within an explicitly prepared demo context.
- Keep keyboard navigation, active/expanded states, readable labels, mobile behavior, and existing role access intact. Grouping pages must not grant unauthorized access, combine Workspace permissions, or make every ordinary forecasting task look like technical configuration.

### Price experiment and acceptance

- Keep public M5 data and artifacts separate from Store databases, Products, approvals, and model directories. The initial price experiment uses the existing reproducible CA_1 / FOODS selection of 50 Products and the unchanged sales target, model family, and declared reference recipe.
- The inspected M5 source contains sell_price on 39,433 of 40,700 selected Product-days, with 1,267 missing prices and observed variation for 31 of 50 Products. It contains no verified stockout or product-promotion flags. Preserve missing prices; do not infer promotions from price drops, public events, or SNAP, and do not infer stockouts from zero sales.
- Compare the existing 14-input reference, the historical-price candidate, and the weekly baseline on identical dated folds, Products, horizons, eligible labels, quantity postprocessing, and metrics. Keep data selection and any feature choices confined to the permitted training/validation evidence. Test promotion and historical-stockout inputs separately later; combinations require separate evidence rather than inheriting a single-feature result.
- The accepted starting feature-value gate is at least a 5% relative reduction in pooled 28-day MAE versus the reference model, improvement in 28-day MAE in at least two of three chronological validation periods, and no more than a 5% relative increase in pooled 7-day or 14-day MAE. Report RMSE, WAPE, coverage, per-Product errors, and systematic underprediction alongside the pass/fail result. Existing Store quality gates remain additional requirements, not replaced by this experimental rule.
- When reference MAE is zero, do not divide by zero or report a relative improvement. A zero-error reference cannot establish a further 5% improvement at 28 days; record the outcome as no demonstrated improvement. At a protected shorter horizon with zero reference error, only zero candidate error satisfies the no-regression condition. Undefined WAPE and missing coverage remain explicit and cannot pass a required gate.
- Freeze the comparison recipe and starting thresholds before evaluating a fresh untouched confirmation period. Previously inspected final M5 windows are development evidence, not fresh confirmation. If untouched evidence is unavailable, report that limitation and do not claim a confirmed improvement or activate the feature operationally.
- Save a reproducible experiment report with input provenance, selected Products, date windows and cutoffs, missingness/variation counts, feature definitions, model/version/parameters, package versions, fold/confirmation assignments, daily reference/candidate/baseline predictions, per-horizon and per-Product metrics, gate outcomes, and reasons for rejection or inconclusive results. Explain results in plain English; a feature-importance chart alone is not evidence of improvement.
- A successful public price experiment authorizes further Store-specific evaluation, not Store deployment. Keep collecting genuine local prices, stock availability, and promotions; each proposed operational feature still requires compatible training, Store checks, and local Administrator approval.

## Testing Decisions

- Use the already agreed end-to-end workflow as the primary test seam: authenticated staff actions and scheduled CLI execution through the existing persisted job/history/model/result boundary. Observe committed state and rendered behavior rather than adding a new forecasting service or mocking away the complete pipeline. The earlier accepted end-to-end reliability check establishes the expected test scope.
- Prefer existing queue/worker, history-export, scheduler, activation, replenishment, and authenticated browser fixtures. Add focused lower-level evidence checks only where they establish a distinct boundary such as cutoff reproduction or intraday stock reconstruction. Test external behavior, not private methods, SQL spelling, Random Forest tree layout, or copied implementation logic.
- Extend the existing scheduler and worker-boundary prior art to cover jobs due at 01:00 Manila, Sunday training with new history, startup before/after the due time, multiple days/weeks off, same-day repeated ticks, simultaneous ticks, database startup delay, pending work, and one-current-run catch-up without duplicates.
- Inject temporary failures, ordinary permanent errors, missing prerequisites, worker crashes, expired leases, and errors between artifact creation and database commit. Assert the shared three-attempt limit, correct terminal/blocked outcome, no concurrent lease ownership, no partial successful output, preserved approved artifacts/results, and a visible deduplicated local notice.
- Exercise the real history reader/export and feature consumer with dated fixtures: open/stocked zero sales, unknown stock, no calendar evidence, a full-day stockout, positive sales before selling out, sellout followed by restocking, an overnight zero balance while closed, missing opening balance, imported zero without availability proof, late refunds/reversals, pack conversion, and price/promotion revisions recorded after the origin. Assert classification, provenance, exported values, and no invented zero labels or lost-demand quantities.
- Demonstrate 140 accumulated usable records across stockout/unknown gaps without resetting the total or compressing dates. Confirm that eligibility can still be blocked by insufficient current inputs or holdout coverage, that excluded days do not become targets, and that a new/ineligible Product does not prevent mature Products from running.
- Reuse the chronological feature/M5 leakage tests and source-parity checks. Changing future sales, future stockout flags, future realized prices, or a promotion recorded after the origin must not alter earlier features or forecasts. Verify that origin-known price observations can affect the candidate while unsupported fields remain unknown.
- Through authenticated role tests, show that an Administrator can review and approve only a qualified Store candidate locally, reject it, pause/resume guidance, and recover a compatible prior model. Verify missing thresholds/metrics, failed gates, incomplete prospective observation, demo artifacts, remote mutation, CSRF failure, and incorrect Workspace all refuse the action server-side. Super Administrator technical setup stays distinct from ordinary Store work.
- Test the first installation end to end: no approved model, too little history, explicit stock-rule fallback, automatic eligible candidate training, automatic observation forecasts without operational promotion, completed qualification, local approval, then a fresh operational forecast. Synthetic elapsed observation is test evidence only, never actual Store approval.
- Exercise forecast completeness, dates, model identity, readiness, suspension, and observation eligibility through the purchasing output. In every unusable case, both displayed expected sales and the recommended purchase calculation must use the appropriate unknown/fallback state; old forecasts remain dated history. Check supported compatible upgrades without erasing history.
- Validate feature-value calculations with deterministic fixtures for the 5% improvement boundary, two-of-three-period requirement, 7/14-day 5% regression boundary, zero-error references, undefined WAPE, matching populations, missing price values, rounding, and unavailable fresh confirmation. These tests prove the evaluation rule, not that price improves real sales forecasts.
- Use existing browser workflow/navigation prior art for both Administrator Workspaces, Inventory Manager access, mobile layouts, keyboard use, active group states, real destination permissions, and absence of duplicate/prototype/demo-only production entries. Capture readable pass/fail and not-ready states rather than relying only on menu source inspection.
- Complete an XAMPP installation verification on a disposable copy before claiming deployment readiness: Windows reboot without sign-in, local service startup, configured executable paths, database delay, PC off across daily/weekly schedules, restart catch-up, a successful full worker run, injected failure/recovery, and local Administrator approval/pause/recovery. Record what was actually run; do not present a Laragon test or fixture render as XAMPP verification.
- Keep tests isolated from live Store records and preserve developer/user working-tree changes. Operational acceptance requires the correct target environment and end-to-end evidence; model accuracy requires separate legitimate chronological sales evidence.

## Out of Scope

- Proving Store prediction accuracy from the small current development database, public M5 performance, or deterministic tests.
- Automatically adding all three operational fields to an approved model without demonstrated value, or claiming price benefits before the experiment runs.
- Training a promotion or stockout predictor from fabricated flags, event calendars, SNAP indicators, receipt purchasing patterns, synthetic inventory, or zero sales alone.
- Estimating the quantity of lost sales, reconstructing customers who never purchased, or changing the target to unconstrained customer demand.
- Automatic model activation without local Administrator approval; routine developer Super Administrator approval after deployment; general platform powers for the Administrator.
- Automatic purchasing, stock changes, purchase-order workflows, supplier optimization, or a redesign of the existing stock-rule and package-rounding policy.
- A second production model family, Flask service, request-time training, remote forecasting API, cloud scheduling dependency, multi-store model, or remote developer access.
- Requiring the PC to stay powered overnight, guaranteeing processing while it is unplugged, or treating PC downtime as proof of Store closure.
- Replacing general backup, recovery, migration, promotion eligibility, calendar management, or Windows infrastructure outside what is necessary for this local forecasting workflow.
- Destructive data cleanup, resetting the Store, silently replacing old imports, or using a public experiment to approve a Store model.
